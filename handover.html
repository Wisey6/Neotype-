Neotype Studio · Owner's guide

Running Neotype

Everything the website does on its own, everything it needs you to do, and what to check when something looks wrong. No developer required for any of it.

01What you have

Four things, and they're already talking to each other.

The website

neotype.au — stickers, banners and corflute, each with a live price calculator. Customers design, upload their art and pay by card without emailing anyone.

Your dashboard

neotype.au/admin — orders, enquiries and every price on the site. It isn't linked from anywhere and search engines are told to skip it.

Stripe

Takes the money and is the record of it. Refunds, payouts and disputes all live in Stripe, not in your dashboard.

Your inbox

Every order and enquiry lands in kiko@neotype.au. Hitting reply writes back to the customer as Neotype.

What happens the moment someone pays

Without you touching anything: the order is recorded with their artwork attached, you get an email, and the customer gets their own confirmation saying a real person will send a proof. That's the promise the site makes on your behalf — the rest of this guide is you keeping it.

02Getting into the dashboard

Two ways in, so forgetting the password is an inconvenience rather than a phone call.

The password

Go to neotype.au/admin and type it in. Tick Stay signed in on this device on your own phone or laptop and you won't be asked again. Don't tick it on anything shared — anyone who picks that device up is straight into your orders.

If you've forgotten it

Click Forgotten the password? Email me a code instead. A six-digit code arrives at your studio inbox, you type it in, and you're signed in for thirty days.

The code only ever goes to your own address — nobody can ask the site to send it somewhere else. It expires after ten minutes, works once, and dies after five wrong guesses.

Worth doing once, today

Try the emailed code before you need it. It's the only way back in if the password is ever lost, and five minutes now beats discovering a problem on a Friday afternoon.

03Working an order

Every paid order moves through four stages. The dashboard shows which stage each job is at and how long it has left, based on the turnaround the customer actually paid for.

1

New

Money cleared, artwork attached, nothing done yet. Download the print file and check it. When the proof is away, press Send proof.

2

Proof sent

Waiting on the customer. When they say yes, press Mark approved. Nothing goes to the press before this.

3

Approved

Cleared to print. Once it's in the post, press Mark shipped.

4

Shipped

Done. It drops out of your open work and stays in the list.

Orders that aren't paid

Most cards clear instantly. A few payment methods take days to settle, so an order can appear before the money has. Those are marked and have no download button at all — the file is deliberately out of reach so a job can't be printed on habit.

Finding an old job

The search box above the order list takes anything you remember — a reference, a customer's name, part of their email, a size, a finish. Typing two things narrows it rather than widening it, so kelly holo finds Kelly's holographic job and nothing else.

04Artwork files

The one thing that bites later

Uploaded artwork is kept for 90 days, then deleted. That's fine for a job you print this week and a problem for a reprint six months on.

Save the file with the job when you download it. Treat the website as the delivery van, not the filing cabinet.

The dashboard warns you three weeks out, on the order itself, and tells you how many days are left. Past the deadline it says the file has gone and to ask the customer to send it again.

If a customer paid but never uploaded anything, the order says so plainly — chase them for it rather than guessing.

05Changing your prices

The Pricing tab. Everything is editable, every figure updates live as you type, and nothing takes effect until you press Save.

The first three boxes in the sticker section are the ones you'll actually reach for: Rate per m² for large runs, the small-run premium per m² added on top of it for short jobs, and the minimum order. Everything below them — finishes, cuts, turnarounds — is a percentage adjustment on that rate.

Change a number and the example prices beside it move immediately, so you can see what a real order would cost before committing. Save, and the website charges the new price on the very next order — there's no waiting and nobody to ask.

When you can't print something

Every finish, shape and turnaround has an on/off switch. Turn one off — the laminate ran out, the cutter's down — and it disappears from the website within seconds. It also becomes genuinely unbuyable: even a customer with the page already open can't order it.

Turn it back on when stock lands. Nothing else to do.

06Volume pricing — your decision

Built, loaded with sensible numbers, and switched off until you say otherwise.

Today's pricing charges by area, which is why a 50 mm sticker costs nearly the same each at 2,000 as at 100 — the thing you flagged. The alternative is a flat rate per cm² that steps down as the order grows, and it's sitting in the Pricing tab ready to go:

Against roughly $20 per m² of vinyl, ink and machine time, the bottom band still leaves about two-thirds gross. It's a volume price, not a giveaway.

Read this before you switch it on

A rate per cm² can only see how much vinyl an order uses. It can't tell a 50 mm sticker from a 125 mm one. So switching over makes small stickers cheaper and large ones in small quantities dearer:

The Pricing tab shows this same before-and-after table for a dozen real orders, recalculating as you edit. Nothing changes on the website until you tick the switch and press Save.

Your call

Leave it off and prices stay exactly as they are.

Switch it on and you undercut the market on small stickers, where you were least competitive, and charge properly for the vinyl a big sticker actually uses.

Or split the difference — drop the 100–249 band to 1.00 and the 4″ rise disappears. One number, and the table beneath shows you the result before you save.

How much a customer can order

Anything from 15 to 5,000 stickers, typed in freely rather than picked from a list. Above 5,000 the site tells them to ask you for a trade price — that's about half a roll of vinyl and worth a conversation rather than a card payment.

What size they can order

Four one-tap presets — 50, 75, 100 and 125 mm square — plus Custom size, which opens a width and a height box. Any rectangle from 10 mm to 300 mm on each side.

Check these against your cutter

10 mm and 300 mm are provisional — nobody has measured what the machine will actually hold. Both are editable in Pricing → Stickers → Advanced settings.

Too small and the site turns away work you could do. Too large and it sells a job you'd have to ring the customer back about. Worth two minutes at the bench.

07When something looks wrong

No order email arrived

Check the dashboard first. If the order is there, the sale is safe and only the email failed — check your junk folder before worrying.

A customer says they paid and got nothing

Search their email in the order list. If it's marked Awaiting payment, their bank hasn't released it yet — tell them not to pay again.

The price on screen looks wrong

Open the Pricing tab and read the example figures. What they show is exactly what a customer's card is charged — the two cannot disagree.

Money questions

Refunds, payouts, disputes and receipts are all Stripe, not the dashboard. Stripe is the record for anything involving money.

The rule of thumb

If an order is in your dashboard, the job exists and the customer's money is accounted for. Everything else — an email that didn't arrive, a page that looked odd — is cosmetic by comparison and can wait until Monday.

08Two things to do this week
